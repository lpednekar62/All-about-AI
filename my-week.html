Why the day opens with a 15-minute ritual

You told me there is no hour when you think best — when you are focused, your thinking is sharp whatever the time or place. So this schedule does not try to catch a peak. It protects against the real enemy, which is starting badly and being interrupted.

The 10:30 block is the on-ramp: numbers, three things, phone down. It is short on purpose. Its only job is to make the 10:45 block actually begin — because on the days it does begin, the rest of your day follows.

Two small things that make the evenings actually work

The 4:45 close is not optional. You said you get home still overloaded and lose the evening scrolling. A day that never formally ends stays open in your head, and an open day cannot be rested from — so the mind reaches for the easiest off-switch. The fifteen minutes at 4:45 is what closes it.

Walk your CBO calls. The 12:45 block is 45 minutes of talking on the phone, six days a week. It does not need a chair. That is four and a half hours of walking a week at no extra cost in time — the only version of exercise that survives a 37-hour week with 8 PM commitments.

Six rules that make it stick

The grid is easy. These are the part that decides whether it survives week two.

1 · The 10:45 block is sacred

Phone face down, door shut, ninety minutes. If you protect one thing on this page, protect this. Everything else can flex.

2 · Messages have three windows

12:15, 3:30, and a short sweep before evening. Outside those, only a phone call reaches you — tell the team that, once, clearly.

3 · Five CBOs, every day, no exceptions

Six days a week is 120 conversations a month. Each ends with a number and a date, or it did not happen.

4 · Meetings only at 2:00

When someone asks for time, the answer is "2 o'clock". Twenty minutes unless there is a real reason. This keeps mornings whole.

5 · The 3:00 team block is never cancelled

It is the first thing that will feel skippable and the last thing you should skip — it is what eventually gives you back hours.

6 · Leave at 5:00, having closed the day

Fifteen minutes to score the day and write tomorrow's three. Then stop. The evening is not overflow space.

Run it for two weeks before you change it

Some blocks will not survive contact with a real week, and that is fine — but change them after two weeks, not on day three when the first thing goes wrong. Keep a note of which block kept getting taken; that note tells you more about where your time actually goes than any plan can.

The one test at the end of a week: did the CBO number move, did Student Genomics move, and did you leave at 5:00 most days? If yes, the week worked, whatever else happened.
