# The Unfinished Decision — 60-Minute DNA Merit Session

A complete, run-ready session design for **customers who have already made a part
payment toward the Welocity Advanced DNA programme but have not completed the
balance or the testing process** — plus a documented adaptation layer for cold
prospects in the same room.

Built from the *Know Your DNA* webinar transcript (Stan Serrao) and the compliance
voice already established in this repository's DNAWellCode assessment.

---

## Read this before you use anything in here

Three things about this pack you need to know up front.

### 1. Every commercial number is a `[VERIFY]` token

The brief asked for a "current product verification table" built from twelve
sources. Exactly **one** of those was available: the webinar transcript. The
PPTX referenced in the brief sits on a local Windows path that this session
cannot reach, and no product sheet, price list, phenotype schedule, evidence
handbook or approved-testimonial file exists anywhere in this repository.

So every product fact in this pack is tagged one of:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[VERIFY]` | Taken from the transcript. **Not** confirmed as current. Do not put on a slide until signed off. |
| `[SUPPLY]` | No source at all. Someone must provide it. |
| `[SAFE]` | Generic science or process language that holds regardless of product specifics. |

**§4 is the gate.** Fill that table, sign it, then build slides. A deck that
quotes an out-of-date phenotype count to people who already paid is worse than
no deck.

### 2. The transcript is a source of ideas, not of claims

§3 audits it line by line. Several of its strongest passages have to be dropped
outright, including some that are load-bearing in the original talk. The two
that matter most:

- **The "small sins" frame.** The transcript explains disease as accumulated
  *paap* — small daily sins that build up and express as illness. It is vivid,
  and it blames sick people for being sick. The brief explicitly bans guilt.
  Cut it.
- **The security-failure analogies.** Naxalite landmines, the Pahalgam attack,
  the World Trade Center. Real attacks with real casualties, used as sales
  metaphors. In a paying audience someone in that room has a connection to one
  of those events. Cut all three.

### 3. This session is designed for one audience

The brief asks for part-payment customers *and* complete new prospects. Those
need different sessions, and running one blended session weakens both — see
**§2.6**. What is provided instead: the part-payment session as primary, with a
six-touch adaptation layer so a cold attendee is never made to feel they walked
into someone else's meeting, and a separate CTA lane for them.

## The one-line summary

Sixty minutes that never says "complete your payment," and ends with the
audience asking themselves *"why am I still delaying something I already decided
was important?"* — then makes the next step small, dated and easy.
