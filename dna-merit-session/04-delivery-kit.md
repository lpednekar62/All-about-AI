# §29 · Audience Interaction Plan

Eight interactions. Each has one job. None exists to "keep energy up" — a
session that needs energy management has a content problem, not an interaction

| **I7** | 18 | 0:55 | Optional chat | *"चाहें तो एक शब्द में लिख दीजिए."* | Voluntary disclosure only. Gives the 1:1 team a routing signal | 45 sec |

## Rules

**Private beats public, three times over.** I4, I6 and I8 are the three that
matter most, and none of them asks anyone to say anything. Public disclosure of
a delay reason in front of peers produces the socially acceptable answer
("busy", "money"), not the true one. Private reflection produces the true one —
which is the only one they can act on.

**Never rescue a silence.** Presenters fill gaps at about four seconds. The
eight-second silence at I6 is the single most important instruction in this
pack. Count it on your fingers, out of shot, if that is what it takes.

**Never call anyone out by name.** Not once, not warmly, not as encouragement.
One person named in a session about an unpaid balance and the entire room
becomes defensive.

**Read chat answers aloud, and never editorialise them.** "Doodh… rice… chai
after 6… curd at night" — then move on. No "ah, interesting, that's actually
because…". The moment answers get graded, the next interaction dies.

# §31 · Testimonials Placement

## Status: BLOCKED

No approved testimonials exist in any available source. The transcript
references Google reviews (1:14:49) but quotes none. **Do not build this slide
until §4 row 30 is filled with written, consented testimonials.**

If they are not available by session day: **run the session without them.**
An absent testimonial slide costs almost nothing here. An unapproved or
overstated one is a compliance incident and, with this specific audience, a
credibility incident too — a stalled customer reads a glowing claim as exactly
the kind of over-selling that made them hesitate.

## Placement when approved

**Slide 16, immediately after the counselling value point.** Three, maximum
five. Six seconds each.

That position is deliberate: the testimonial should evidence *understanding*,
which is the value claim being made on that slide — not the decision to buy,
which is not being asked for yet.

## Selection criteria

Choose ones that demonstrate:

- **Useful awareness** — "मुझे पता चला कि…"
- **Counselling clarity** — "counsellor ने समझाया तो…"
- **Personalised understanding** — "अब मुझे पता है कि मेरे लिए…"
- **Decision confidence** — "अब मैं guess नहीं करता"
- **Service experience** — process, responsiveness, support

## Reject regardless of how good they sound

Any testimonial claiming: a disease was cured or reversed · medication was
stopped or reduced · a specific weight loss figure · a diagnosis was made ·
a health outcome was prevented · a doctor was replaced · any dramatic
transformation.

**The instinct will be to lead with the most dramatic one available. Resist it.**
A dramatic testimonial does two bad things at once: it makes a claim the
programme cannot support, and it raises expectations that will produce a
disappointed customer in month four. A quiet "the counselling finally explained
something I'd wondered about for years" converts better with a sceptical
audience and survives scrutiny.

## Required treatment for every testimonial

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Consent | Written, on file, specific to presentation use |
| Attribution | First name + city only. No photo without separate consent |
| Length | Under 25 words on screen |
| Disclaimer | **"Individual experiences vary and do not guarantee the same outcome for others."** — on the slide, legible, every time |
| Editing | Trim only. Never rewrite, never compress two into one |

## Template

> *"[Quote, under 25 words, about understanding — not about outcomes.]"*
> — [First name], [City]
>
> Individual experiences vary and do not guarantee the same outcome for others.
