01Executive Diagnosis

Six findings. The first three come straight out of your spreadsheet and change what you should do on Monday morning.

Finding 1 · You are not short of CBOs. You are sitting on 900 kits nobody is selling.

Your CBO roster has 106 CBOs, of whom 93 have already taken kits in advance — 1,196 kits in total. You told me only 20–22 are meaningfully active. That means roughly 72 CBOs are holding around 900 already-committed kits and doing nothing with them.

You have been treating this as a recruitment and activation problem. It is a sell-through problem. Those kits are already paid for, already in the field, already costed. Getting those 72 CBOs to move 10 kits each over seven months is 720 kits — 35% of your entire ASO+CBO gap — with zero new recruitment, zero new inventory and zero new spend.

Nothing else in this plan has a return anywhere close. This is your Theory-of-Constraints bottleneck, and it has a name and a phone number attached to each of the 72.

Finding 2 · Student Genomics has not stalled. It has stopped.

SGO booked 10 kits in April, 8 in May, 8 in June — and then zero in July and zero in August. Total 26 against a 1,200 target. This is not a channel running slowly; it is a channel that has flatlined for two consecutive months while carrying ₹2.03 Cr of the annual plan.

A line that produces nothing for two months is not behind on effort. Something structural is broken — no owner driving it, no lead source, or no offer that converts. Diagnosing which of those three it is, is a Week-1 job, not a Month-2 job.

Finding 3 · You own 48% of the plan and are delivering 15% of the revenue.

ASO+CBO (₹4.99 Cr) and SGO (₹2.03 Cr) together are ₹7.02 Cr — 47.7% of the ₹14.71 Cr plan — and the workbook names LAXMAN against both. In August the company booked ₹1.03 Cr. Your two lines contributed 84 kits and zero: ₹15.1 lakh, about 15%.

The other 85% came from Mumtaz's CCNG and UPG/UPS engine, which is broadly tracking. So the honest read is this: the parts of the business you monitor are working, and the parts you own are not. Your calendar is currently inverted against your accountability.

Finding 4 · Your incentive design pays the team to ignore your biggest KRA

You told me F-Sale carries the highest points, so Jayshree drops Associate and CBO work to chase it — and she is behaving rationally. ASO+CBO is 2,045 kits and ₹4.99 Cr, the single largest revenue line in the company plan. Your scoring system currently rewards people for walking away from it. Re-weighting the points table costs one meeting and nothing else; leaving it costs the year.

Finding 5 · You are the company's poster factory, and there is a designer on the payroll

You estimate 2–3 hours at a stretch on creatives, repeatedly, because "the others don't know how and don't have a ChatGPT subscription." Fiza is a graphic designer sitting in your office. The blocker is not talent and not tooling — it is that no content calendar and no brief format exist, so every poster arrives as a fresh emergency that only you can absorb. Two AI seats cost roughly ₹4,000/month. Your time at the 85% incentive band is worth considerably more than that per hour.

Finding 6 · The political geometry is unresolved, and it will decide whether any of this survives

Mumtaz has two years more tenure, a direct and older trust line to Stan sir, and — by your own reading — resentment about reporting to you. She runs CCNG competently. That combination means every structural change you make can be re-framed upward before you get to explain it. This is not managed by being careful. It is managed by making her win visibly and by routing credit toward her deliberately. Section 13 covers the mechanism.

The one-line diagnosis

You are spending your scarcest resource — 37 hours — producing creatives for channels other people own, while 900 paid-for kits sit dormant in your own network and your second-largest line has been dead for two months.

Two cliffs, and you should aim at them deliberately

Crossing 80% → 85% costs ₹75 lakh of revenue and pays +₹3.94 lakh. Crossing 90% → 95% costs the same ₹75 lakh and pays +₹4.50 lakh. But 85% → 90% pays only ₹0.94 lakh for the identical effort.

So landing on 84% or 94% is expensive, and the bands just above each cliff are where your effort is worth roughly five times more. Aim at 85%, then at 95%. Do not aim at "as much as possible" — it is a worse strategy than aiming at a rung.

Why there is no "peak energy" block

You said there is no particular hour when you think best — that when you are focused, your thinking is sharp regardless of time or place. That is unusual and it is useful: it means you are not energy-limited, you are interruption-limited.

Genuinely urgent — interrupt anything
